Company Overview

Learn about Xiaowei Health Technology's company positioning, parent company strength, and brand portfolio

Xiaowei Health Technology (Shenzhen) Co., Ltd. is a high-tech medical device and service company under Skyworth Group, dedicated to the integrated R&D, production, and marketing of smart wearable devices, acoustic and hearing health systems. The company focuses on cutting-edge technology R&D in the health smart wearable industry, covering areas such as cardiovascular and cerebral health monitoring, early warning and intervention, hearing assistance, hearing acoustics, and artificial intelligence. By aggregating resources from the Tencent Tianlain Lab, Skyworth's R&D, manufacturing and distribution capabilities, and Ping An Health services, it provides wearable health system solutions for health-conscious consumers and people with hearing impairment.

Xiaowei Health Technology is a company under Skyworth Group (00751.HK / Skyworth Digital 000810.SZ). Founded in 1988, Skyworth Group ranked 380th on the 2025 China Top 500 Enterprises list, with businesses covering four major sectors: smart home appliances, smart system technology, new energy, and modern services. Xiaowei Health Technology carries Skyworth Group's strategic layout in the health smart wearable and acoustic hearing fields.

Xiaowei Health Technology owns two major brands: 1) SKYWORTH — health smart watches (adult watches, children's phone watches) and smart Bluetooth headphones; 2) Bigsound — AI Chinese hearing aids. The two brands share Xiaowei Health Technology's R&D, production, acoustic algorithm, and medical service resources.

Xiaowei Health Technology was founded by Wang Hai, who serves as Chairman. Hearing Health Education

Learn about hearing loss classification, impact, and intervention options

Hearing loss is classified by World Health Organization (WHO) standards: 1) Normal (0-25dB HL); 2) Mild (26-40dB HL): difficulty hearing soft speech; 3) Moderate (41-60dB HL): difficulty hearing normal speech, hearing aids recommended; 4) Severe (61-80dB HL): difficulty hearing loud speech, hearing aids required; 5) Profound (>80dB HL): barely hears any sound, requires powerful hearing aids or cochlear implants. Hearing loss is irreversible, but timely hearing aid use can prevent progression and is the most effective intervention.

Age-related hearing decline (presbycusis) is a common physiological phenomenon, with 1/3 of people over 65 having hearing impairment. However, "common" does not mean "normal and requires no intervention". People with hearing impairment are 5 times more likely to develop cognitive disorders, and hearing damage is one of the most important factors in dementia (accounting for 1/4). Regular hearing checks are recommended for seniors, and timely hearing aid use is advised for moderate or greater hearing loss to reduce the risks of dementia, falls, and depression.

Hearing damage has a major impact on the current and future quality of life of older adults: 1) Dementia: People with hearing impairment are 5 times more likely to develop cognitive disorders, with hearing damage as the most important factor accounting for 1/4; 2) Increased falls: People with 25dB mild hearing loss fall 3 times as often as normal, with each 10dB decrease increasing fall probability by 1.4 times; 3) Depression: 8.36% of people with hearing impairment have chronic diseases, and 30% have depression. Without timely action, global annual losses reach 981 billion USD, equivalent to the Netherlands' annual GDP.

According to WHO data, 1.6 billion people worldwide had hearing loss in 2019, accounting for 1/5 of the global population. In 2021, China had 70 million+ people with bilateral moderate hearing loss, projected to reach 100 million and 130 million by 2035 and 2050 respectively. Globally, 430 million people with moderate or greater hearing damage need hearing aids, and by 2050 the global hearing loss population is expected to reach 2.5 billion. China's hearing aid market potential will exceed 120 billion yuan.

The hearing aid adoption rate among people with hearing loss in China is less than 5% (95% do not wear them), far below developed countries such as France/UK, US, and Japan. Reasons include three main aspects: 1) Low user awareness: 67% of seniors do not know their hearing has declined, and only 7.4% of ear disease patients in China have received hearing tests; 2) Low government awareness: Children's subsidies are well-established, but only Beijing/Shanghai/Guangdong/Fujian and a few other cities offer subsidies for seniors; 3) Industry conditions: Insufficient domestic attention, foreign product and technology monopolies, and high purchase costs.

Most hearing impairment is irreversible (sensorineural hearing loss), but timely hearing aid use can prevent progression and is the most effective intervention. With timely hearing aid fitting and hearing rehabilitation services, the vast majority of people with hearing impairment can have their hearing compensated, participate in social activities like people with normal hearing, and improve their quality of life. The WHO targets a 50% hearing aid adoption rate by 2030, and China still has a significant gap.
